# BAN-6001
Evaluating College Value and Affordability in the U.S. Higher Education System: A Data Analytics Project
Beginning with a raw federal higher-education dataset spanning thousands of institutions and variables, this project cleans and structures the data into 1,407 schools. We explore this data descriptively with summary statistics and visualizations, model student outcome probability using normal and empirical distributions, raise formal hypothesis tests and confidence intervals on tuition and earnings benchmarks, and close with a regression model to refine the variable set and address multicollinearity to better explain what actually predicts college cost and outcome measures.


DATA
Source:U.S. Department of Education
Topic:College Value Contributors
Size:10 Continuous variables, 3 categorical variables, 1407 rows
Research Question:Our team selected the College Scorecard dataset because, as graduate students, we are interested in what variables contribute to students going on to earn more or less in their careers. We chose this data over other options because its variables capture multiple distinct aspects of an institution. We hope to identify which institutional characteristics are most strongly associated with median earnings 10 years after enrollment. One angle we are specifically interested in is cost versus payoff: how much students spend on school compared to how much they have earned since attending.
